## 0. What is there today (`current-932x430.png`, measured)

No game state is on the screen except the bike chip and the ticker times.

Why it now reads as stale next to the other two screens: the garage and the level select are *places* rendered by the game — the BE3 bay with its wet floor and sodium lamp, the diorama tile on its water table — with the UI as objects in them (tags on a rail, a clipboard, pins on posts, a card that belongs to the tile). It also sits in a fixed state: no last run, no up-next, no career, no medals — everything the diorama card shows the moment you tap PLAY.

## 2. The five directions

### A — "Bay" (`A-bay.jpg`)

**What it is.** You are standing in the garage. The BE3 set exactly as it is live — the shutter half open on the dusk skyline, the IRONWORKS stencil and BOLT ENERGY sticker, the sodium lamp left, the wet floor, pegboard, a NORDVIK drum and KESTREL TYRES stack right — seen a little wider and lower than the garage's own orbit camera, the hero side-on on its stand right of centre in the lamp pool. The left edge is the garage's steel rail with four shop tags hanging from it, CREDITS at the top and GARAGE at the bottom so the lowest tag is under the left thumb. PLAY is one amber slab in the bottom-right corner reading `PLAY` / `UP NEXT · M1 HOP UP · MEDIUM` / `INDUSTRIAL · target 1:10.000`. Top-right under the chip, a charcoal `LAST RUN` card (E3 Stairway · silver 1:01.300 · Canyon · Pro · target, `6 / 15 CLEARED`, four medal dots). Badge plate top-left, ticker along the bottom.

**What it does with the brief.** The most literal answer to "fit the garage": it *is* the garage, and the tags and rail are the garage's own controls re-purposed as the menu's secondaries, so GARAGE → garage is a camera push-in, not a screen change. One big / four small / one card — round 1's A "Paddock" hierarchy, now on the set that actually shipped.

**Mockup vs brief.** Element for element. The hero is the right bike (blue-white tank) and the right outfit (charcoal hoodie, open-face helmet, jeans). Tags are stacked from the bottom as asked; the PLAY slab is angled like the badge plate, which the brief did not ask for but which ties it to the wordmark. Only the drawn frame is taller than the phone (1.92 vs 2.17): read the bay ~12 % shorter.

**844×390.** The four tags are ~80 px each on screen in the drawing; at 390 px tall with the badge above and the ticker below they need ~60 px pitch — fine, the garage already stacks six tags in that height. The LAST RUN card and the PLAY slab do not meet at 932 and will not at 844 (the card ends at ~45 % height). The rail's top tag (CREDITS) sits under the badge at 844: drop it to a text link on the rail's spine if it collides.

**What it costs against `front.ts`.** Medium, and mostly *already paid*. The set is `setGarageStage(true)` (live for the garage, wired in `src/main.ts` `onGarageStage`) plus a fixed `setCameraOverride({ mode: 'orbit', … })` at a wider dist / lower screenY than the garage's rest pose — no new renderer work, one camera preset. The hero is the live rider, so the outfit and bike follow the garage for free (today's key art shows neither). The menu keeps its `FocusList`; the five items render as one `.menu-play` slab plus a `.menu-rail` of tags (the garage's `.tag` rules in `styles.ts` restyled for four items), and the LAST RUN card is a new `.menu-last` reading `bestOf()` + `nextTrack()` which the screen already receives. The key-art path (`.menu-keyart`, `art.applyBackground`) goes; the ticker stays. Risk: the garage runs at a 30 fps cap with the stage on and the menu would inherit it — acceptable on the menu, but the stage's ≤ 18 draws plus the hero is the boot screen's cost from the first frame (today's boot draws key art over an idle b1). Estimate: one day.

## 4. Recommendation

**A, "Bay", as the direction — with E's full-width band as the one thing to steal if the user wants a bolder PLAY.** Reasons, in priority order:

1. **It is the ask.** "Fitting the garage / level select style" is answered most directly by the screen *being* the garage's set with the garage's rail and tags: one material system across menu → garage → (PLAY) → the diorama's card. B fits the level select instead and pays for it with the tallest panel; C is a still; D is a new scene; E fits the garage's light but not its furniture.
2. **Cheapest honest path, and the layout is right before the art is.** The stage, the orbit camera and the tag styles are all live (`setGarageStage`, `orbit`, `.tag`); A is one camera preset, a slab, a rail and a card — a day — and the hero is the live rider on the live bike, so the outfit and bike class the user picks in the garage are on the home screen from the next boot. Today's menu shows a different rider on a different bike.
3. **The hierarchy is round 1's brief, finally on a set.** One big amber PLAY that says what it will play, four small tags under the other thumb, one card that says what happened last — fewer buttons than today with more hierarchy, and every control in a thumb corner.
4. **It composes with what the user already picked.** BE3's lamp and the amber UI are one hue (the garage SPEC's own point), and A's frame is BE3's frame one step back.

Pick **E** if the priority is the boldest possible first impression — the hero huge and the name the size of a title — and accept the 844 chrome squeeze (§ 2 E) and the hero-up-close open item. Pick **B** if the priority is *time to riding*: it is the only direction that puts the next track's card on the boot screen, so PLAY is "ride Hop Up" without a second decision — and its scene is the cheapest of the five (b1's own start line, re-aimed). Pick **D** if the three screens becoming one continuous world is worth two to three days, and budget the pan. **C** is the best-looking still and the one the user should look at for *materials* (the pit board, the chalk, the timing sheet) even if another architecture wins; on its own it gives up the live hero.

Whichever wins, the round that ships it needs a played clip on the phone harness: cold boot → home screen with the seeded state → PLAY under the thumb → the diorama card → RIDE → countdown, with a stranger's first tap landing on PLAY, the home screen's first frame drawn within the boot budget the loader reports, and the menu-stills e2e (`harness/e2e/menu-stills.mts`) re-pointed at the new layout at 932×430, 844×390 and 1280×720.
