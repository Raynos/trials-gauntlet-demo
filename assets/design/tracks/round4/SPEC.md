## 1. What is there today (measured on the shipped build)

`TrackSelectScreen` (`src/ui/front.ts:420-830`) with the data in `src/ui/trackMap.ts`: **one horizontal snap scroller of six pages** — `island` (Lab + five Playgrounds), `industrial`, `canyon`, `snow`, `nightCity`, `foundry` — each page one isometric night plate (`art/tiles/tile-<page>.webp`) with that biome's tracks as pins, the amber route through them, the medals as lit trophies on the front ledge, and a `NEXT TIER GATE → HARD · H1 ROOFTOP WIRE · MEDAL EVERY MEDIUM TRACK` stub on the campaign pages. A 44 px **miniature row** along the bottom (`LAB · INDUSTRIAL 3/4 · CANYON 3/3 · SNOW 0/2 · NIGHT CITY 0/2 · FOUNDRY 0/4`) snaps between pages; a swipe on the map does the same; no arrows are drawn. Keys / pad: ←→ walk the pins and spill to the next page, ↑↓ step pages. The focused pin's card (RIDE · ▶ GHOST · REVIEW · top-5) rises on the page's right. The screen opens on the last-played track if still open, else `nextTrack()` (M1). This is the "individual islands, their own pages" the user means: **each biome is a page; nothing of the next biome is visible from the current one** except its miniature.

Counted from the menu (PLAY = tap 1; a tap on an unfocused pin focuses, the focused pin or RIDE launches):

| | Pages | Taps · swipes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start **B1** | opens on page 2 (Industrial) | **2** when B1 was last played (PLAY · RIDE); **3** otherwise (PLAY · B1 · RIDE) | the seeded state opens focused on M1 unless `lastTrack` is B1 |
| Start **M1** | page 2 | **2** (PLAY · RIDE — M1 is the opening focus when nothing was last played) | same page as B1 |
| Read **H1's rule** | page 2 | **1** (the `NEXT TIER GATE` stub on the opening page names H1 and the rule) | — |
| Reach **H1's pin** | page 5 (Night City) | **3** (PLAY · NIGHT CITY miniature · H1) or 2 + **3 swipes** (Canyon, Snow, Night City) | a tap on H1 shakes and states the rule on the card |
| Reach **X2 / X3** | page 6 (Foundry) | 3 (PLAY · FOUNDRY miniature · pin) or 2 + 4 swipes | — |
| Lab → Foundry | 1 → 6 | 5 swipes | the whole world is 6 screens; at most one is ever visible |

So the paging cost is not the tap count — the miniature row already makes any page 1 tap away — it is that the world is never *seen*: 6 discrete screens, the seams are cuts, and the route (the career) is chopped into per-page segments. That is what the three directions change.

## 3. Per direction

### A — "Ribbon" (`A-ribbon.jpg`)

**What it is.** The five slabs fused into one raised landmass on the water: the NORDVIK headland at the left (`LAB · PROVING GROUND · NO MEDALS`, `LAB · PHYSICS TEST`, five flags `P1 CONTAINER YARD · OPEN` … `P5 FOUNDRY FLOOR · OPEN`), then Industrial (IRONWORKS, VORTEX OIL tank, cranes; B1 gold focused with its card `B1 · BEGINNER · INDUSTRIAL / FIRST RIDE / Best 0:41.200 · Target 1:05.000 / ▶ GHOST · 2 / 6 NOTED`, B2 silver `LEAN BACK 0:47.900`, B3 bronze `KICKER ROW 0:58.100`, both `▶ GHOST`), a rail bridge into Canyon (E1 silver `UPHILL WEIGHT 0:52.400`, E2 bronze `REAR WHEEL FIRST 1:06.800`, E3 silver `STAIRWAY 1:01.300 · PRO`), M1 dashed with the amber `UP NEXT` flag at the Canyon / Snow seam, the snow slope with M2 `DRUM ROLL` and a lit cabin and chairlift, and at the right edge the hazard-tape barrier `HARD · MEDAL EVERY MEDIUM TRACK / NEXT UNLOCK · H1 ROOFTOP WIRE` with Night City's neon and the `RIDE FURTHER` sign glowing beyond it. `INDUSTRIAL · 3 / 4`, `CANYON · 3 / 3`, `SNOW · 0 / 2` engraved on the cut face. The road is lit amber B1 → E3 and dashed to M1. Below the map a full-width strip: the whole island in miniature (headland · Industrial · Canyon · Snow · Night City · Foundry, each with its name and count), every pin as a dot, padlocks from H1 on, the amber bracket over the left 45 %.

**What it does with the brief.** The most literal answer to the ask — the shipped tiles, un-paged. The seams are the win: the Industrial cliff turns to sandstone in one continuous cut face, the canyon's top is dusted into the snow, and the barrier at the seam makes "locked" a *place* you pan up to rather than a greyed page. The strip is round-2 A1's legend strip, redrawn as the world itself — it is both minimap and progress bar and it is the one thing on the screen that shows the whole career at once.

**Mockup vs. brief.** Frame 1536×802 (1.92:1 — near the 2.17:1 spec, read the map band ~12 % taller than the phone). Deviations: **M1 is drawn at the Canyon / Snow seam, not on Industrial** — the generator straightened the route's doubling-back (B3 → E1 → E3 → M1 → M2 reads left to right; the brief asked for M1 at the Industrial / Canyon seam with a dashed return). The strip is ~72 screen px tall, not 44 (it is the better height — see § 5). Text: the strip lists `X1` twice (once in Snow, once in Night City — the second should be H2/H3 territory); E2's `1:06.800` is red where every other under-target time is green; the playgrounds are flags rather than pads. Complete otherwise: Lab, five playgrounds, nine pins with PB / target, the barrier with the rule, both `▶ GHOST` tags, the PRO chip, `UP NEXT`, the card. No off-frame problem by design — the strip carries the rest.

**844×390.** The map band loses ~40 px; the landmass is one plate scaled to fit, so the pins stay 44 px and their plates (name + PB / target) start to collide where four sit within one biome (Industrial). Same fix as round 3's A3a: code + disc only when unfocused, the plate on the focused pin. The strip at 44 px is fine; at its drawn 72 px it costs the map band 28 px — worth it (§ 5).

Markers B1 (gold, focused, the card), B2, B3, M1 (`UP NEXT`), E1, E2, E3 (`PRO`), M2, with name and PB / target boxes; region names lettered on the paper with pictograms (`INDUSTRIAL · 3 / 4` with a crane, `CANYON · 3 / 3` with a mesa, `SNOW · 0 / 2` with a peak, `NIGHT CITY 0 / 2` with a padlock). The index-map strip below shows the whole sheet — the five regions, every marker, padlocks, the `EXTREME · MEDAL EVERY HARD TRACK` barrier, the P3–P5 pennants, BOLT ENERGY at the Foundry end — with the amber rectangle over the left 40 %.

The relief-in-paper trick keeps the diorama the user liked while giving the seams a neutral medium to blend through: where A has to invent terrain that is half rust and half sandstone, B's regions can simply feather into contour lines.

**Mockup vs. brief.** The first run drew a **3:2 screen filling the whole canvas** (1536×1024, no letterbox) — codex's own note: *"The generated image omitted the requested black letterboxing"* — so read that image as ~45 % taller than the phone: at 19.5:9 you see the map from row 1 to row 3 and the index strip, or the map is scaled to two-thirds. Text: `MEDIMM` on the barrier label (run 1). **844×390.** Markers are smaller than diorama pins (the head is the 44 px disc; the label box hangs below), so B is the most tolerant of the height loss: the map band at ~230 px still holds two regions with their markers and label boxes; the title block and the grid letters are the first things to drop at 844 (they are decoration). ## 5. Build notes against `TrackSelectScreen` (`src/ui/front.ts:420-830`) and `src/ui/trackMap.ts`

(The task named `src/ui/tiles.ts`; that file is the pause / results action `TileRow` and is untouched by any of these — the diorama is `front.ts` + `trackMap.ts` + `styles.ts`'s `injectTrackMapStyles`.)

Common to all three: the six `.tpage` snap pages become **one scene** in a single scroller — `.tmap` keeps `overflow-x: auto` but drops `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory`, the six `.ttile` plates are laid along one strip (A, B) or one column (C) with their anchors in *world* space instead of tile space (a `PAGE_ORDER[i].offset` added to `Pin.x/y` in `trackMap.ts`), the route becomes one SVG polyline across the strip (today's per-page `route` / `routeLit` concatenated), the miniature row `.tmini` becomes the minimap strip (same `button.tm` per region, plus a bracket element positioned from `scrollLeft / scrollWidth`, and the row's tap → `gotoPage` becomes a smooth `scrollTo`), the focus model stays (`page`, `pinIndex[]`, `nav()` — a page is now a *region*, and ←→ already spills across regions), and `snapTo` becomes "scroll so the focused pin is in the middle third". The card, the gate stub (`.gate`), the locked shake, keys / pad, `currentPage()` for the harness — all unchanged. The art is the cost: the six plates must be re-cut so their edges **blend** (A, C) or **feather into paper** (B) instead of ending in a cut slab; that is a codex image-pipeline job per seam, not code.

## 6. Recommendation (for the user to accept or reject)

**Build A (Ribbon), with B's index-map strip and its hatch for locked regions.** In order:

1. **It is exactly the ask.** "One continuous world map of these biomes, combined" — A is the shipped tiles with the seams filled in, nothing reinterpreted. The user chose the diorama over the painted map in round 2; A keeps that choice.
2. **It fits the phone the way today does.** One horizontal axis, the same swipe, the same miniature row (grown into a strip that now shows *where you are*), pins at 44 px, the card unchanged; the 844 story is round 3's known fix (plates only on the focused pin).
3. **The strip is the real gain.** Today the six miniatures are a tab bar; in A the strip is the whole career in one glance — every medal, every padlock, the lit stretch of road, the window — and it is what makes a 3–4-screen world navigable in one tap. Draw it at the mockup's ~72 px, not 44: that height is what makes the dots legible.
4. **It is the cheapest honest build**: the common refactor above plus five seam plates; no new interaction, no new axis.

Second pick **B** if the user wants the map to *feel* like a map rather than a model — its paper is the cheapest way to blend seams and the most Broadcast surface of the three, but the mockup shows the relief swallowing the chart, so it would need one more round to find its balance. **C** only if progression-as-climb matters more than the horizontal thumb: it is the best picture and the worst 844 fit, and it inherits A3c's vertical-axis problem unchanged. Whichever ships, settle the route-order question in § 4 first (tier-order road with a return spur to M1, or biome-order road with tier on the pin) — it decides the seam plates.
